AI Job Hunter

An open-source Python CLI that discovers jobs from Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, screens them against a configurable candidate profile, and produces explainable recommendations. An optional Gemini assessor compares technical requirements with documented candidate experience.

Overview

Job discovery combines public Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby postings with a configurable candidate profile. The CLI separates eligibility decisions from fit ranking and produces explainable Markdown reports rather than hiding uncertainty behind a score.

Architecture and data flow

ATS APIs → validated and deduplicated job records → deterministic eligibility → optional Gemini technical assessment → SQLite tracking and Markdown reports. Pydantic validates job data and structured assessment output. Hard restrictions remain authoritative when results are combined.

Engineering decisions

Model output is treated as untrusted input. Employer quotations are checked against the job description, and candidate quotations against recorded experience. Valid provenance establishes where text came from; it does not establish that the text proves proficiency. Missing or invalid mandatory-skill evidence requires review.

Implementation and reliability

Deterministic gates handle eligibility separately from technical assessment. A deterministic rejection stays rejected, and review cannot be promoted by AI. Bounded retries and partial discovery recovery prevent a single source failure from silently defining the whole search. Structured logs and latency and token-usage reporting support diagnosis.

Testing and evaluation

Version-controlled job snapshots, reference labels and a candidate profile drive evaluations through production decision logic. Offline deterministic runs are separate from optional live Gemini regressions. Saved JSON artifacts make individual decisions comparable. The documented baseline matched 12 of 12 curated deterministic labels and passed 4 of 4 focused live regression cases; these small datasets do not establish accuracy on unseen postings.

Outcomes and lessons

The project demonstrates a working boundary between deterministic policy and probabilistic assessment. Explainability requires preserving source evidence and uncertainty, not merely requesting a model explanation. Broader reference datasets and repeated live runs would strengthen evidence of generalization.
